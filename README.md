# QualHub Demo: Opening the Black Box

This is a demo repository for the session "Presenting Data in Assignments".

It shows how a qualitative study can share its analysis trail without sharing any personal data.

The study here is invented for teaching. It looks at why small farms in North East Scotland start new business activities, such as farm shops or holiday lets.

## The rule

**Transcripts stay in SRUC OneDrive. GitHub holds the thinking, not the people.**

No names, no transcripts, no audio and no consent forms go in this repository.

## What is in each folder

| Folder | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `01_raw_data_NOT_HERE` | Nothing. A note tells you where the raw data is kept and who can access it. |
| `02_codebook` | The list of codes, what each one means, and an example of when to use it. |
| `03_memos` | Short notes written during analysis. They show your thinking. |
| `04_analytic_log` | A dated record of decisions, such as merging or renaming codes. |
| `05_instruments` | The interview guide used in the study. |
| `06_figures_tables` | Tables and figures used in the report. |

## How a reader uses this

1. Read a finding in the dissertation.
2. Check the codebook to see what the code means.
3. Check the memos and log to see how the theme was built.

This lets a reader follow your analysis step by step, which is what makes research transparent.

## How to use this as a template

Click **Use this template** at the top of the page. This makes your own copy. Then replace the demo files with your own.

Keep your repository **private** unless your supervisor agrees it can be public.

## Codebook format

The codebook is given in two forms:

- `codebook.csv` opens in Excel.
- `codebook.qdc` is the REFI-QDA codebook format. It opens in ATLAS.ti and NVivo, and it holds codes only, with no data.

Do not upload QDPX project files. They include your source documents.
